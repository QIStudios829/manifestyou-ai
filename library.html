The source texts.

Scripture and contemplative writing from the world's traditions, plus every text referenced in Adventures of Gradient Descent. Complete and free to read.

Hindu & Vedic

Dharma, the self, and the discipline of attention.

  • The Bhagavad Gita
    Translated by Sir Edwin Arnold · 1885

    Krishna counsels Arjuna on the battlefield: act fully, release the outcome.

  • The Upanishads
    Translated by Swami Paramananda · 1919

    The philosophical core of the Vedas: Atman, Brahman and the nature of the self.

  • The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali
    Translated by Charles Johnston · 1912

    The classic manual of meditation and the stilling of the mind.

  • The Vedanta-Sutras
    Translated by George Thibaut, with Shankara's commentary

    The foundational text of Vedanta philosophy, read through Shankara.

  • The Mahabharata
    Translated by Kisari Mohan Ganguli · Volume 1

    The great Indian epic that holds the Bhagavad Gita. Later volumes are on Project Gutenberg too.

  • Raja-Yoga
    Swami Vivekananda · 1896

    Vivekananda's commentary on Patanjali, written for Western readers: the mind as the instrument of freedom.

  • Karma-Yoga
    Swami Vivekananda · 1896

    Work without attachment to results. The path Gradient can't escape: "Karma Yoga doesn't let you pick your dharma."

  • Addresses at the Parliament of Religions
    Swami Vivekananda · Chicago, 1893

    The speeches that opened with "Sisters and brothers of America" and brought Vedanta to the West.

Buddhist

The path out of suffering, through clear seeing.

  • The Dhammapada
    Translated by F. Max Müller · 1881

    The Buddha's teachings in short verses on mind, conduct and freedom.

  • The Gospel of Buddha
    Compiled by Paul Carus · 1894

    The life and teachings of the Buddha, gathered from early sources.

Taoist & Confucian

The way of nature, and the way of right relationship.

  • Tao Te Ching
    Translated by James Legge · 1891

    Laozi's eighty-one short chapters on the Tao, softness and non-action.

  • Chuang Tzu
    Translated by Herbert A. Giles · 1889

    Zhuangzi's parables of freedom, perspective and the butterfly dream.

  • The Analects of Confucius
    Translated by James Legge

    Sayings of Confucius on virtue, learning and how to live among others.

Abrahamic

Scripture of the Jewish, Christian and Islamic traditions.

  • The Bible
    King James Version · 1611

    The Hebrew Bible and the New Testament in the classic English translation.

  • The Bible
    Douay-Rheims Version

    The Catholic English translation, including the deuterocanonical books.

  • The Quran
    Yusuf Ali, Pickthall & Shakir translations, side by side

    Three English renderings of each verse, set next to one another.

  • The Koran
    Translated by J. M. Rodwell

    A complete 19th-century English translation.

Mystics & Contemplatives

Inner practice across traditions.

  • The Mesnevi
    Rumi · translated by James W. Redhouse · 1881

    Rumi's great Sufi poem of longing, love and return.

  • Confessions
    Saint Augustine · translated by E. B. Pusey

    An inward autobiography and one of the first great works of introspection.

  • Meditations
    Marcus Aurelius · translated by Meric Casaubon

    A Stoic emperor's private notes on discipline, impermanence and calm.

Hermetic & New Thought

Where the Law of Attraction came from: "Hermetic alchemists whispered it, transcendentalists preached it."

  • The Kybalion
    Three Initiates · 1908

    The best-known modern summary of Hermetic philosophy, including "As above, so below."

  • Nature
    Ralph Waldo Emerson · 1836

    The founding essay of American Transcendentalism: spirit read through the natural world.

  • Essays: First Series
    Ralph Waldo Emerson · 1841

    Includes "Self-Reliance" and "The Over-Soul," the shared mind behind every individual one.

  • Walden
    Henry David Thoreau · 1854

    Two years in a cabin, living deliberately. Includes "Civil Disobedience."

  • The Science of Getting Rich
    Wallace D. Wattles · 1910

    The New Thought classic on thought shaping circumstance: the old ancestor of the Manifest YOU spec.

Philosophy & Psychology

The thinkers behind the book's cages, shadows and dead gods.

  • The Critique of Pure Reason
    Immanuel Kant · 1781

    The source of the a priori: the "Kantian cage" Gradient's heart overfit to.

  • The Joyful Wisdom (The Gay Science)
    Friedrich Nietzsche · 1882

    Where "God is dead" first appears. A diagnosis, the book's glossary notes, not a triumph.

  • Thus Spake Zarathustra
    Friedrich Nietzsche · 1883

    Nietzsche's answer to nihilism: create new values from the wreckage of the old.

  • Fathers and Sons
    Ivan Turgenev · 1862

    The novel that brought the word "nihilist" into literature, through Bazarov.

  • The Interpretation of Dreams
    Sigmund Freud · 1899

    Freud's map of the unconscious and repression: what Arg and Kwarg call "tech debt of the soul."

  • Psychology of the Unconscious
    C. G. Jung · 1912

    Jung's break with Freud, and the start of his work on archetypes and the collective unconscious.

The Machine Canon

The papers behind the characters. Free to read on arXiv.

  • Attention Is All You Need
    Vaswani et al. · 2017

    The paper that introduced the Transformer. He would like you to know he read it. Loudly.

  • Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization
    Kingma & Ba · 2014

    The bartender's origin story: per-parameter learning rates from the moments of the gradient.

  • Batch Normalization
    Ioffe & Szegedy · 2015

    The technique that calmly stabilized deep training. It sends ReLU postcards.

  • Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition
    He et al. · 2015

    Residual connections: sometimes the most useful thing a layer can do is nothing.

  • Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models
    Hoffmann et al. · 2022

    The Chinchilla paper. Not about the forest chinchilla, who was not cited.

Also in the Book

Referenced in Adventures of Gradient Descent but still under copyright, so there's no free full text to link. Find them at your library or bookshop.

  • Slaughterhouse-Five
    Kurt Vonnegut · 1969

    The source of the book's refrain: “So it goes.”

  • The Master and Margarita
    Mikhail Bulgakov · written 1928–1940

    The novel behind “Reason died at dawn.”

  • The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy
    Douglas Adams · 1979

    Why 42 is the random seed in nearly every machine learning tutorial.

Books link to public-domain editions on Project Gutenberg and Wikisource. Papers link to arXiv. All free to read online.
